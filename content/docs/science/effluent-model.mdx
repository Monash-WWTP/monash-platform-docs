## Reading the equations

These equations describe `effluent_v1` version 1.4.0 exactly as implemented. They explain a deterministic heuristic; mathematical notation does not establish calibration or predictive validity. Read the [parameter register](/docs/science/parameters) alongside the equations for coefficient provenance.

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit / interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| $i$, $n$ | Day index (starting at zero), horizon length | $n$ is 30, 90 or 180 days |
| $m$, $w_i$ | Demand multiplier, weekly modulation | Dimensionless |
| $Q_0$, $Q_i$, $K$ | Assumed baseline flow, daily flow, configured capacity | MLD (million litres/day) |
| $a_i$, $p_i$, $c_i$ | Aeration, pump, clarifier availability | Fractions from 0 to 1 |
| $B$, $C$, $S$ | BOD₅, COD, TSS surrogate states | mg/L |
| $H$, $O$, $R$ | Ammonia, organic nitrogen, nitrate surrogate states | Nitrogen concentration convention of the input/output; mg N/L |
| $A$ | Nitrification activity | Dimensionless, bounded to [0, 1] |
| $\Delta t$ | Numerical substep | 0.25 of a day; four updates per day |

Define $\operatorname{clip}(x,l,u)=\min(\max(x,l),u)$. Numerical constants below are implementation coefficients; they are not independently established physical kinetic parameters. The code blocks are matching pseudocode, not separate scientific models.

## Time and input resolution

Horizons are 30, 90 or 180 days, starting at the requested UTC date. Demand percentage takes precedence when supplied: `m = max(0, 1 + demand_change_pct/100)`. Otherwise the model uses a custom truthy multiplier or low/normal/high values 0.85/1/1.25.

The requested demand change $d$ is a percentage. When present it takes precedence over other demand settings:

$$
m=\max\left(0,1+\frac{d}{100}\right),\qquad
w_i=1+0.06\sin\left(\frac{2\pi i}{7}\right).
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_i &= Q_0 m w_i, & X_{\mathrm{in},i}&=X_0\sqrt m,\\
K_i &= Kp_i, & u_i&=\frac{Q_i}{\max(K_i,10^{-6})}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $X_0$ is the assumed baseline concentration for each incoming constituent. The weekly wave affects flow only; the square root affects concentrations. The small denominator floor prevents division by zero and does not imply usable capacity when pumps are unavailable.

```text
weekly(i) = 1 + 0.06 × sin(2πi/7)
flow(i) = assumed_flow × m × weekly(i)          [MLD]
concentration_multiplier = sqrt(m)
hydraulic_capacity(i) = capacity_mld × pump_availability(i)/100
utilization(i) = flow(i) / max(hydraulic_capacity(i), 10⁻⁶)
```

Maintenance applies on `start_date ≤ day < start_date + duration_days` and lowers the unit's availability to the minimum active event/baseline value. Weather and rainfall are stored but do not drive these equations.

## Four bounded surrogate updates

Initial concentrations use the **unscaled** assumed influent: nonnegative BOD, COD, TSS and ammonia; organic nitrogen is `max(TKN − ammonia, 0)`. Nitrate starts at zero and nitrification activity `A` at one. Daily incoming concentrations are multiplied by `sqrt(m)`.

For each day, `a` and `c` are aeration and clarifier availability fractions. Let `h = max(utilization, 0)`, `o = 1/(1 + max(h − 1, 0))`, `e = min(h, 4)` and `dt = 0.25`. Apply the following sequence four times per day; each expression uses the state updated by preceding lines.

### Initialization and hydraulic modifiers

$$
\begin{aligned}
(B,C,S,H)_0 &= \max\bigl((B,C,S,H)_{\mathrm{assumed}},0\bigr),\\
O_0 &= \max(\mathrm{TKN}_0-H_0,0), & R_0&=0, & A_0&=1,\\
h_i&=\max(u_i,0), & e_i&=\min(h_i,4),\\
o_i&=\frac{1}{1+\max(h_i-1,0)}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The first maximum is componentwise. States begin at the unscaled baseline and carry forward between days. $e_i$ controls exchange with incoming concentrations; $o_i$ reduces the subsequent transformations above capacity. Neither is a hydraulic residence time.

### Ordered substep equations

The arrows below are assignments: **each line uses the values left by all previous lines**. Repeat the sequence four times for each day. For $X\in\{B,C,S,H,O\}$, first update activity, exchange and carbon decay:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &\leftarrow A+0.45\Delta t(a_i-A),\\
X &\leftarrow X+\Delta t\,e_i(X_{\mathrm{in},i}-X),\\
R &\leftarrow R-\Delta t\,e_iR,\\
B &\leftarrow B\exp[-\Delta t(7a_i+0.2)o_i],\\
C &\leftarrow C\exp[-\Delta t(4a_i+0.2)o_i].
\end{aligned}
$$

Next transfer organic nitrogen into ammonia, then ammonia into the nitrate surrogate. $M$ and $J$ are temporary transferred amounts, not extra persistent states:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= O\bigl(1-\exp[-0.3\Delta t\,o_i]\bigr),\\
O &\leftarrow O-M, & H&\leftarrow H+M,\\
J &= H\bigl(1-\exp[-2.5\Delta t\,A o_i]\bigr),\\
H &\leftarrow H-J, & R&\leftarrow R+0.85J.
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally calculate the bounded denitrification surrogate $D$ using the updated BOD and nitrate:

$$
f_B=\min\left(\frac{B}{\max(B_{\mathrm{in},i},1)},1\right),
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \min\left(R,\Delta t\,0.8(1-0.4a_i)o_iR f_B\right),\\
R &\leftarrow R-D, & B&\leftarrow\max(B-0.25D,0).
\end{aligned}
$$

Afterward, replace each concentration with its maximum with zero and clip $A$ to [0, 1]. The nitrate yield 0.85 and BOD consumption 0.25 are heuristic choices; these updates do not establish nitrogen or carbon mass conservation.

```text
A += dt × 0.45 × (a − A)
X += dt × e × (X_in − X)    # each of BOD, COD, TSS, ammonia, organic N
nitrate += dt × e × (−nitrate)
BOD *= exp(−dt × (7a + 0.2) × o)
COD *= exp(−dt × (4a + 0.2) × o)
mineralized = organic_N × (1 − exp(−dt × 0.3 × o))
organic_N -= mineralized
ammonia += mineralized
nitrified = ammonia × (1 − exp(−dt × 2.5 × A × o))
ammonia -= nitrified
nitrate += 0.85 × nitrified
denitrified = min(nitrate,
  dt × 0.8 × (1 − 0.4a) × o × nitrate × min(BOD / max(BOD_in, 1), 1))
nitrate -= denitrified
BOD = max(BOD − 0.25 × denitrified, 0)
```

After each substep, clamp every concentration and activity to at least zero, and cap activity at one. The daily overload term used below is `clip(utilization − 1, 0, 1)`. BOD, COD, ammonia and nitrate outputs are the final states; TSS receives the clarifier adjustment below.

## Derived outputs and screens

Let $v_i=\operatorname{clip}(u_i-1,0,1)$ be the daily overload fraction. At the end of the fourth substep:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{\mathrm{out},i}&=S_i(1-0.95c_i),\\
P_{\mathrm{out},i}&=\max\bigl(4.5(1-0.75c_i)+0.8+1.5v_i,0.3\bigr),\\
T_i&=\max(0.55S_{\mathrm{out},i}+1,0.5),\\
\mathrm{pH}_i&=7.2-0.30(1-a_i).
\end{aligned}
$$

TSS and phosphorus outputs use mg/L, turbidity $T_i$ is reported in NTU, and pH is dimensionless. These are algebraic output mappings with implicit coefficient units, not independently validated conversion laws. The remaining reported concentrations are their final surrogate states.

```text
TSS_out = TSS_state × (1 − 0.95 × clarifier_fraction)
P_out = max(4.5 × (1 − 0.75 × clarifier_fraction) + 0.8 + 1.5 × overload, 0.3)
turbidity = max(0.55 × TSS_out + 1, 0.5)
pH = 7.2 − 0.30 × (1 − aeration_fraction)
```

Daily exceedance is output greater than its configured/fallback limit. The combined screen counts days where any checked metric exceeds its limit; pH is skipped in that KPI loop. Fallback values are BOD 50, COD 200, TSS 100 mg/L. The combined percentage is `100 × (1 − mean(daily_any_exceedance))`. For capacity utilization, clip **each daily ratio** to [0, 2] before taking the mean, multiply by 100 and round to one decimal. This screen does not represent an uncapped physical capacity measurement.

### Screening and capacity summaries

For configured checked metrics $\mathcal M$ (excluding pH in the combined KPI), define the daily indicator $z_i$ using the strict greater-than comparison:

$$
z_i=\mathbf{1}\!\left[\exists j\in\mathcal M:\ Y_{ij}>L_j\right].
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
N_{\mathrm{exceed}}&=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}z_i,\\
S_{\mathrm{screen}}&=100\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}z_i\right),\\
U_{\mathrm{summary}}&=\frac{100}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\operatorname{clip}(u_i,0,2).
\end{aligned}
$$

$Y_{ij}$ is output metric $j$ on day $i$; $L_j$ is its configured or fallback threshold in the same unit. Equality to a threshold is not an exceedance. The combined percentage counts days passing every checked threshold, rather than averaging individual metric percentages. Capacity is clipped **before averaging**; daily capacity-series values can still exceed 200%. Both percentage KPIs are rounded to one decimal in the returned result. A screening pass is not evidence of permit compliance.
